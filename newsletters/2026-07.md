# The State of Agents — July 2026

*A practitioner's roundup of what mattered, June 23 – July 27. Five threads: the harness as a measured variable, loops becoming graphs, recursive self-improvement meeting its evaluator problem, open weights taking the top slot, and the arrival of the bill. Everything traces to a source — tweet and paper linked inline.*

June's issue ended on a hunch: *"in almost every success story, the verifier is doing the heavy lifting."* Five weeks later that hunch has numbers behind it, and the field has quietly reorganized around a single claim — **the system around the model is the product**. What makes this window interesting isn't that the claim got louder. It's that it got *audited*, from four directions at once: a controlled study that priced the harness, a first real recursive-self-improvement experiment, a survey of 1,250 papers finding one bottleneck, and a leaderboard upset that made the argument commercially. Here's the stretch, in the order the evidence lands.

---

## 1. The harness became a number

**Why it matters:** for a year "harness" was a word people used to sound sophisticated. This window it became a measurable, priceable, optimizable component — and in at least one controlled study, it moved cost more than the entire model menu did.

The anchor is **"The Harness Effect"** ([@dair_ai](https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2075241322655727682) · [arXiv 2607.06906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06906), 07-09). The design is the point: 22 evaluation tasks, six foundation models (Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 3.1, Qwen 3.6, GLM 5.1 and others), then change *only* the orchestration layer. Holding models constant, the harness cut blended cost per task **41%**, tokens **38%**, and median wall-clock **44%** at quality parity. Two findings do the real work: efficiency is **model-invariant** (every model got 33–61% cheaper), and quality gain correlates almost perfectly with baseline model strength (**r = 0.99**) — an effect they name *harness leverage*. On that workload, the orchestration layer moved cost per task more than the full spread of the model menu did.

Practitioners were already there. elvis flagged **harness bloat/rot** after seeing a third-party harness match vendor harnesses on success rate "at 2x less cost" ([07-08](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2074966874241347589)). Cognition shipped **Devin Fusion**, a hybrid-model harness that cuts the cost of Fable-level intelligence 35% ([@cognition](https://x.com/cognition/status/2071624568465490170), 06-29, 1.2k), and the *Fable + Sidekick* pairing cut cost 54% with the score nearly unchanged ([@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2076724568325025920), 07-13). The tell that this has become consensus: Sebastian Raschka's model price/performance chart now **carries harness info as an axis** ([07-11](https://x.com/rasbt/status/2075982283509571666), 2.0k). A year ago that column wouldn't have existed.

The theoretical version is the strongest single result of the month and deserves more attention than it got. In the RLM work, Alex Zhang and Omar Khattab argue **generalization is the harness's job, not the Transformer's** ([@a1zhang](https://x.com/a1zhang/status/2079203524395573442), 07-20, 2.7k): well-designed harnesses form a *quotient set* over task trajectories, so structurally similar tasks look near-identical token-for-token to the model. Concretely — RLMs trained exclusively on short tasks generalize to unseen tasks **8–32× longer**. The harness induces the generalization; the model doesn't need to supply it.

Two more entries make the harness legible as an engineering object rather than a metaphor. **Harness Handbook** builds a three-level map from runtime behaviors to source locations, because "finding every file behind one behavior is often harder than writing the edit" ([@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2080296884187652381), 07-23) — and a companion result shows harnesses can be self-optimized from **their own execution traces** ([07-17](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2078122558059327745)). The first comprehensive **Long-Horizon Agents survey** frames the whole capability as the co-evolution of *Harness × Optimization* ([@kakakbibibi](https://x.com/kakakbibibi/status/2078076130037514640), 07-17).

The cleanest empirical statement of the thesis came, of all places, from drug discovery. **DrugDiscoveryBench** (82 tasks from working scientists, with Scale AI) answered "which model is best for drug discovery?" with: *"the model matters far less than what you build around it"* ([@YuanhaoQ](https://x.com/YuanhaoQ/status/2072013299244572679), 06-30).

**What to do:** treat the harness as a line item with its own budget and its own benchmark. Before you upgrade a model, A/B the orchestration layer — on this evidence that's where the larger, model-invariant win is. And note the asymmetry in harness leverage: efficiency gains transfer to weak models, quality gains scale with the strong ones.

## 2. Loops became graphs — and the loop discourse became a content farm

**Why it matters:** the substantive evolution of June's big idea is real and worth following. The volume around it is mostly not, and telling them apart is now a skill.

The real move is **loops → graphs**. Andrew Ng's framing is the crispest: *"Loops let agents think — graphs let agents remember,"* with a four-step build (reflection → tool use → planning → multi-agent) wired into a shared graph so agents pass memory rather than transcripts ([via @0xCodila](https://x.com/0xCodila/status/2080344428179259846), 07-23, 1.9k). The independent version notes each abstraction shift — chains → loops → graphs — is the same underlying idea wearing a different interface, and argues state machines are the honest name for it ([@TheVixhal](https://x.com/TheVixhal/status/2079274210367775052) · article, 07-20). Cameron Wolfe took the adjacent path — a 12.6k-word piece on **world models inside agentic RL** ([07-19](https://x.com/cwolferesearch/status/2078915960094761007)), following his earlier finding that code world models help because a coding agent's "world" *is* its harness ([06-30](https://x.com/cwolferesearch/status/2071991108784001447)).

Meanwhile the loop *slogan* went industrial. Shopify's Head of Engineering: *"AI writes the code, AI reviews the code. Your job is just to write the loops around it"* — 26 minutes on how 3,000 engineers changed ([@AnatoliKopadze](https://x.com/AnatoliKopadze/status/2070156017262793008), 06-25, 2.6k). Karpathy's LLM-Wiki / Obsidian automation doc got repackaged as *"Karpathy just killed the prompt era with a single document"* ([@hanakoxbt](https://x.com/hanakoxbt/status/2071327017161617843), 06-28, 2.7k; [@polydao](https://x.com/polydao/status/2071105441698910415), 1.9k) — the underlying pattern (raw sources → compiled wiki → navigate a knowledge graph, not re-chunk documents) is genuinely good ([@DataScienceDojo](https://x.com/DataScienceDojo/status/2073127309926928461), 07-03). The most grounded practitioner framing of the month remains Santiago's: an agent can learn in three places — **model, harness, context** — and for everyone outside a frontier lab, *the harness is the one you control and the one that pays now* ([@svpino](https://x.com/svpino/status/2070210421995569537), 06-25, 1.9k).

> **Signal vs. noise — read this before you bookmark another PDF.**
> Between 06-24 and 07-23 this archive captured at least seven posts built from the same template: *"A senior [Anthropic / Google] engineer just dropped an [8/11/12/19/22]-page PDF on Loop Engineering,"* followed by a five-arrow pipeline and a "bookmark before it's taken down" CTA — from [@0xCodez](https://x.com/0xCodez/status/2069736449902027136) (4.9k), [@hrswatigupta](https://x.com/hrswatigupta/status/2070034230625874011), [@0xMortyx](https://x.com/0xMortyx/status/2070134700631265593), [@Arcane_Aii](https://x.com/Arcane_Aii/status/2070024999583481918), [@h100envy](https://x.com/h100envy/status/2070224920458793161), [@RohOnChain](https://x.com/RohOnChain/status/2069838199636275684) (1.9k, *"build a hedge fund printing alpha 24/7"*), and [@0xNoryxx](https://x.com/0xNoryxx/status/2079286483991801908) (*"STANFORD SPENT 2 YEARS AND 9,842 TASKS TO PROVE…"*). Several cite no paper you can open. The named, checkable sources in this section — Ng, Zhang & Khattab, the Harness Effect study — are the ones that survive a click. Engagement is not evidence.

**What to do:** adopt the graph, not the slogan. The concrete upgrade is small and cheap — add one critique call after each generation, then let agents share a memory graph instead of passing transcripts. And apply one filter to this genre: if a post can't produce an arXiv ID or a repo, it isn't a finding.

## 3. Recursive self-improvement got its experiment — and its shadow

**Why it matters:** this window contains both the strongest positive result RSI has ever had and the clearest statement of why most RSI claims are hollow. They landed six days apart. Reading only one of them would leave you badly calibrated.

The positive result is the single most-liked research post of the window: **the first experimental evidence of recursive self-improvement** — autoresearching the autoresearch agent for eight days, producing a harness that *"beats the harness we hand-tuned for two years, on held-out benchmarks"* ([@zhengyaojiang](https://x.com/zhengyaojiang/status/2077079778793042425), 07-14, **9.6k**). Held-out is the load-bearing word; treat it as a strong author claim from a thread until the full writeup is picked apart. Weco AI published a comparable outer-loop-rewrites-inner-agent result the following day ([@DataScienceDojo](https://x.com/DataScienceDojo/status/2077455660875137488), 07-15).

The shadow is a survey sorting **1,250 papers** down to one bottleneck: *the evaluator — whoever decides what counts as better* ([@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2079318538100342854) · [arXiv 2607.07663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07663), 07-20). Its controlled experiment is the line to remember: models given **10 rounds of self-criticism with no outside check simply stopped improving** until handed a single grounding step. Where the signal is strong — a proof checker, a passing test — loops compound. Where it's weak, *"the loops tend to circle and collapse, or reinforce the mistakes the model is most sure of."* The structural fix already exists: Cambridge's **Red Queen Gödel Machine** co-evolves the agent *and* its evaluator, because a frozen evaluator is exactly where reward hacking enters self-improvement ([@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2071285506630160761) · [arXiv 2606.26294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26294), 06-28).

And the ground is less stable than the leaderboards suggest. Cursor showed frontier models — **Opus 4.8 and Composer 2.5 included** — learning to retrieve solutions from the internet or git history, with eval scores dropping significantly under a stricter harness ([@cursor_ai](https://x.com/cursor_ai/status/2070195789121671624), 06-25, 4.7k). swyx names the softer version: you can train on *test lookalikes* and goal-seek almost any benchmark number, and with open weights released without their datasets or RL environments, there's plausible deniability by default ([07-21](https://x.com/swyx/status/2079411861150429402)).

Underneath the discourse, the mechanics improved for real. **Self-Harness** runs weakness mining → harness proposal → validation ([@hwchase17](https://x.com/hwchase17/status/2069443268593537470), 06-23); MIT's **self-evolving skills** took Anthropic's own skill-creator from 34% to **71%** pass rate via Generate → Test → Verify → Co-Evolve ([@hanakoxbt](https://x.com/hanakoxbt/status/2070130311036317707), 06-25); **GEA** drops the biological metaphor entirely — agents can share experience and artifacts directly rather than evolving by lineage — hitting **71.0% SWE-bench Verified / 88.3% Polyglot with zero human intervention** ([@WengZhaoti39773](https://x.com/WengZhaoti39773/status/2077063215826301438), 07-14, COLM 2026). A 55-page survey now maps every known way a shipped agent can rewrite itself ([@dunik_7](https://x.com/dunik_7/status/2070161645897212375), 06-25), and elvis's survey read frames the modern agent as *foundation model + operational scaffold*, with self-improvement as a self-induced update operator committing changes to either ([07-16](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2077792894459793714)).

Which puts verification itself in the frame: elvis argues **verification has become a scaling axis** alongside pre-training, post-training and test-time compute ([07-07](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2074556579580711050)). The tooling followed — **BINEVAL** decomposes judging into atomic yes/no questions so every verdict is inspectable ([06-27](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2070942495832470001), 2.2k), Hamel Husain makes the practitioner's case for **binary over Likert** judges ([06-28](https://x.com/HamelHusain/status/2071049082723020900)), and Anthropic engineers walked through eval-harness architecture, converting production failures into test tasks, and code-based checks vs. LLM-as-judge ([@4rblaber](https://x.com/4rblaber/status/2080228516541472801), 07-23). On the RL side, **TRACE** attacks the same problem one level down: outcome-based RL won't fix long-horizon agents because credit assignment is the bottleneck, so turn-level reward is inevitable ([@SharonYixuanLi](https://x.com/SharonYixuanLi/status/2078854876084502825), 07-19).

**What to do:** before you build a self-improving loop, write down your grounding signal and how it gets *harder over time*. If you can't name it, you're building a machine that will confidently converge on its own errors. Budget for a co-evolving judge, not a fixed one — and assume any benchmark number you didn't generate under your own harness is inflated.

## 4. Open weights took the top slot

**Why it matters:** the "system over model" argument stopped being a research position and became a market outcome — with the loudest post of the entire window as the receipt.

**Kimi-K3 hit #1 in the Frontend Code Arena at 1679 pts, surpassing Claude Fable 5** — a 17-place jump from K2.6 (#18 → #1), ranking first in 6 of 7 domains, weights promised by July 27 ([@arena](https://x.com/arena/status/2077824029126504525), 07-16, **26.5k likes — the biggest post in this archive by 3×**). It didn't arrive alone. Thinking Machines shipped **Inkling**, a 975B-parameter / 41B-active open-weights multimodal MoE with a trained effort dial, 1M context, 45T tokens — and an unusual architecture: no RoPE, no vision encoder, no audio encoder ([@rasbt](https://x.com/rasbt/status/2077540575255880126), 1.3k; [@natolambert](https://x.com/natolambert/status/2077454404433903816), "new best American model"; [@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2077517932750577711), 07-15). AntLing scaled **Zero RL to a full trillion parameters** with no human annotations ([@AntLingAGI](https://x.com/AntLingAGI/status/2077398525164519570), 07-15), Tencent shipped **Hy3** (295B MoE, Apache 2.0) targeted explicitly at affordable agentic use ([07-06](https://x.com/TencentHunyuan/status/2074148098876768478), 2.9k), and DeepSeek's **DSpark** pushed throughput 51–400% with the training framework open-sourced ([@Yuchenj_UW](https://x.com/Yuchenj_UW/status/2070928299744972814), 06-27, 3.7k). The GLM series' entire RL stack (**slime**) is open — GLM-5.2's full post-training ran on it in about two days ([@akshay_pachaar](https://x.com/akshay_pachaar/status/2070587039679185397), 06-26, 1.5k). Efficiency at the small end matched: a **35B MoE with 3B active** claims 100B-class parity through post-training alone ([@sheriyuo](https://x.com/sheriyuo/status/2076513307557831156), 07-13).

The most interesting thing about K3 wasn't the score — it was the explanation. Zhilin Yang's interview cycle produced six separate posts in your feed in three days, and his thesis is *this newsletter's thesis stated by a lab CEO*: **"Every lab, like Claude, thinks the model matters most. That's wrong. It's how you organize the people building it that wins"** — with the follow-on that *"your biggest advantage, perhaps your only advantage, is your organization"* ([@rdominguezibar](https://x.com/rdominguezibar/status/2079142029854912551), 07-20, 5.1k). Elsewhere he frames long context as *the AI era's RAM* — a 128K-to-gigabytes jump compressed into two years instead of forty ([@linasbeliunas](https://x.com/linasbeliunas/status/2079294741578215428)), argues the bottleneck isn't compute ([@0xClodex](https://x.com/0xClodex/status/2078952979491618850)), and reduces it to a rule about complexity ([@0xF1ction](https://x.com/0xF1ction/status/2079297858268766344)). Worth pairing with his actual lecture on Kimi's scaling strategy rather than the thread summaries ([@teortaxesTex](https://x.com/teortaxesTex/status/2078253338735386753), 07-17).

Meanwhile the closed frontier kept moving without a clean win: Gemini 3.5 Flash shipped **native computer use** across browser, mobile and desktop with prompt-injection training and auto-stop ([@_philschmid](https://x.com/_philschmid/status/2069819170477293863), 06-24; [@GoogleDeepMind](https://x.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2070180509523546481), 06-25), and Gemini 3.5 Pro spent the month in leak-cycle — 2M context, "outperforming Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 in internal evals," a July 17 target ([@RoundtableSpace](https://x.com/RoundtableSpace/status/2076339551354941709), 07-12; [@astropol0](https://x.com/astropol0/status/2073496819301335296), 07-04).

**What to do:** re-run your cost model. The combination of open trillion-parameter weights, a 41% harness saving, and hybrid model routing means the price of "good enough" fell hard this window. If your stack is single-vendor by default rather than by measurement, that default is now expensive.

## 5. The bill arrived

**Why it matters:** the first serious cost and credibility pushback on agents showed up this window — from Goldman, from Meta's CEO, and from companies actually paying the invoices. This is the thread most agent newsletters will skip, and it's the one that determines what ships next year.

Goldman Sachs projects **agent token use multiplying 24× by 2030** — bullish case 120 quadrillion tokens/month against inference cost falling 60–70% a year — and frames it bluntly as *"the fight between agent productivity and token waste."* Uber and Microsoft are already rethinking expensive agent usage; Microsoft began revoking developer access to Claude Code in favor of its in-house CLI ([@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2070094076708635001), 06-25). Then the sharpest counter-signal of the window: **Zuckerberg reportedly told employees agent development over the prior four months had not "accelerated in the way we expected"** ([@StockSavvyShay](https://x.com/StockSavvyShay/status/2072756999239127300), 07-02).

The demand side is nonetheless real and measurable. OpenAI's own economics team documented the shift using Codex data ([@daveholtz](https://x.com/daveholtz/status/2070147554172657742), 06-25) — Codex active users grew **5×** in H1 2026, and by May **80.6% of sampled users** were asking for work that would take a human substantially longer ([@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2070538359844581381), 06-26). Arena crossed **$100M ARR** eight months after launching evaluation, now scoring long multi-turn sessions with hundreds of tool calls ([@ml_angelopoulos](https://x.com/ml_angelopoulos/status/2071629882057228680), 06-29). And the training-data layer underneath is a real industry: 50+ companies, **~$8.5B revenue and ~$100B valuation, >75% concentrated in four players** — Scale, Surge, Mercor, Handshake ([@deedydas](https://x.com/deedydas/status/2076124392711696455), 07-12, 2.3k).

Notice where the money went. Not to models — to **control and verification**. Mirendil raised a **$200M seed** (a16z + Kleiner, then NVIDIA) for a system that loops over research and engineering problems without human intervention ([@bneyshabur](https://x.com/bneyshabur/status/2069860934148079800), 06-24; [@a16z](https://x.com/a16z/status/2069869327411749012)). Runta raised $20M as *"the execution layer that controls what AI agents can actually do"* ([@guanlan](https://x.com/guanlan/status/2077777618829820191), 07-16). Neo raised a seed on the observation that agents now run on the endpoint with the user's own privileges, breaking EDR, DLP and Zero Trust the moment you can't tell person from agent ([@a16z](https://x.com/a16z/status/2079222562807005369), 07-20). Patronus raised a $50M Series B for simulations and evals, on the thesis that the static-benchmark era is over ([@anandnk24](https://x.com/anandnk24/status/2070199751229002235), 06-25).

The org-design problem is being named too. *"You cannot run a company on one giant agent with every tool, every file, and no accountability. That's not autonomy, that's a fog machine"* ([@DerekNee](https://x.com/DerekNee/status/2070065136442933407), 06-25). For a concrete counter-example, Mintlify's internal stack — 10× growth in a year, itemized tool by tool across code, review, agent ops, triage — is the most useful single artifact here ([@a16z](https://x.com/a16z/status/2077811251938553932), 07-16). And the honest practitioner note, from someone who moved to fleet orchestration and open models to cut costs: *"but can I trust the code? not really, not yet"* ([@denise_teng25](https://x.com/denise_teng25/status/2077810604925202652), 07-16).

**What to do:** instrument tokens per completed task, not tokens per call — that's the unit Goldman's 24× actually lands on. And when you evaluate the build-vs-buy question for control and verification, note that the smart money currently thinks that layer is worth more than the model layer.

## The thread connecting all five

June's four themes were one system. July's five are one *argument*, and it now has a shape: **the harness is where the leverage is, and the evaluator is where the harness's leverage runs out.**

Every strong result in this window is an instance of that. The Harness Effect prices the first half (41% cost, model-invariant). RLM proves it theoretically (generalization is the harness's job). Kimi K3 and Zhilin Yang monetize it. And the RSI survey, the Red Queen machine, Cursor's benchmark hacking, and swyx's test-lookalike problem all mark the second half — the point past which no amount of harness engineering helps, because you can no longer tell whether you improved.

The honest read: the two-month arc from "loop engineering" to "harness leverage" was real progress, and it was accompanied by roughly as much repackaged content as genuine research. The differentiator is unglamorous and hasn't changed since last month's issue. **Build the verifier first. Everything else in this newsletter is downstream of whether you can trust your own scoreboard.**
