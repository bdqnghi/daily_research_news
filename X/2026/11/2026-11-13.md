### @HowToPrompt__ (How To Prompt)
[Tweet](https://x.com/HowToPrompt__/status/2121261265658187947)

> China just got AI to self-improve during pre-training.. something no lab on earth has pulled off before.
> 
> They published a paper that takes recursive self-improvement (RSI) to a terrifying new level.
> 
> Usually, RSI means a model generating data to fine-tune its own weights. but these researchers applied it to the entire organization and infrastructure of a frontier pre-training run.
> 
> And here is why this is mindblowing..
> 
> When you train a massive frontier model, you can't just "rerun" it to test a hypothesis because it costs millions.
> 
> that single run leaves behind a vast ocean of context: internal metrics, infrastructure diffs, hyperparameter changes, and dozens of checkpoint evaluations.
> 
> it is way more data than a human engineering team can actually read, let alone remember.
> 
> So, they built a closed loop where ai agents act as the engineers.
> 
> The agents don't just tweak models. they analyze the failed runs, build data-pipeline components, crawl the web, and generate agentic training data at a scale no human team could ever manage by hand.
> 
> They even fix the infrastructure. during the pilot runs, the agents autonomously diagnosed and resolved five massive cross-stack issues in about a month, tracking problems all the way from optimizer states down to low-diversity synthetic data.
> 
> And the results are insane..
> 
> They used this agentic system to execute a 1T-token pre-training run from scratch, creating a base model they named "801" (after the classroom where it was built).
> 
> by letting the agents continuously revise the data and recipes based on what failed previously, the model's mean score across 22 benchmarks jumped from 47.78 to 51.96, and finally to 56.83.
> 
> What this actually means for the future: rsi doesn't stop at the model anymore. the "improving system" is now the pre-training project itself, the agents, the human researchers, and the infrastructure.
> 
> every time a run fails, the agent records exactly why, turning negative results into persistent, autonomous knowledge so the next loop starts smarter.
