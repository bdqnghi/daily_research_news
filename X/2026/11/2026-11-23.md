### @andrew_li03 (Andrew Li)
[Tweet](https://x.com/andrew_li03/status/2124952540714582496)

> I strongly believe most evals on long-horizon agents are wasting compute.
> 
> Agent trajectories are getting long enough that evaluating them is becoming its own infrastructure problem. Over 20% of trajectories on our platform now exceed 1M tokens.
> 
> The default is to point a frontier model or agent judge at the whole thing, but most of those tokens have nothing to do with the question being asked.
> 
> Take a judge that checks whether a user is frustrated. It doesn't need 100+ tool calls. It needs the user turns, and a few lines of code can find those.
> 
> At @JudgmentLabs, we now:
> 
> 1. Have an agent study your trajectory structure once
> 2. Generate a small script that extracts the evidence each judge needs
> 3. Run a decision model on that evidence
> 4. Fall back to the full LLM judge or agent judge when something looks off
> 
> In production, this has cut eval cost for our customers by up to 80% compared with LLM and agent judges.
> 
> I think this is where evals are heading: cheaper compute to find the evidence, and the smallest model that can make the call.

### @thesupermannx (Superman)
[Tweet](https://x.com/thesupermannx/status/2124755826967740620)

> MIT published a paper that might end the entire RAG industry.
> 
> It’s called MeMo (Memory as a Model).
> 
> Instead of storing your documents in a database, you train a separate, dedicated "Memory" model to internalize them.
> 
> Your main LLM, the "Executive" model, stays completely frozen.
> 
> Your new knowledge gets encoded into a separate, dedicated Memory Model.
> 
> Here’s the clever part. They don't just dump raw text into it.
> 
> A 5-step synthesis pipeline distills your documents into structured "reflections”, extracting facts, surfacing entities, and stitching together cross-document relationships before the Memory Model is trained.
> 
> Then, at inference time, when you ask a complex question, the Executive Model doesn't search a clunky vector database.
> 
> It interviews the Memory Model.
> 
> Through a 3-stage multi-turn protocol, it breaks your question down into targeted sub-queries, pulls the exact synthesized knowledge it needs, and reasons over the final answer.
> 
> Why this changes the game:
> 
> • Zero catastrophic forgetting: Your main LLM’s weights are never modified.
> 
> • True plug-and-play: It treats your main LLM as a black box, no weights or logits needed. It works with both open-source and proprietary closed-source models.
> 
> • Flat retrieval cost: Inference cost stays independent of corpus size, because the memory lives inside a fixed-size model.
> 
> • Built for complexity: Validated across BrowseComp-Plus, NarrativeQA, and MuSiQue, outperforming both RAG and fine-tuning baselines while resisting retrieval noise.
> 
> We’ve spent years trying to build bigger context windows and smarter search bars to patch AI amnesia.
> 
> The real fix wasn’t giving models a bigger notebook.
> 
> It was giving them a second brain.
