### @rohanpaul_ai (Rohan Paul)
[Tweet](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2123828039276773655)

> New Stanford+other top labs paper finds that a 27B open model can improve itself by editing its own weights through reasoning, with no training or gradients.
> 
> Improving a deployed model today means another training run on large clusters. Prompts and extra inference compute help, but the gain disappears once the task ends.
> 
> They gave Qwen3.5-27B its own weights, its graded practice answers, and a Python session, with gradients blocked. It compared its hidden states on answers that finished against answers that ran out of tokens, then weakened the direction separating them.
> 
> Its own edit raised accuracy from 57.2% to 63.8% on 490 unseen problems, in a test fixed in advance. The 2B model made no edit, and the 4B and 9B models broke themselves.
> 
> If you run a large open model, let it propose weight edits for its own failures, but keep only edits that pass held-out tests.

### @askalphaxiv (alphaXiv)
[Tweet](https://x.com/askalphaxiv/status/2123819408577683928)

> We created the world’s first open source benchmark for end-to-end autoresearch. Today, its results are live.
> 
> Which models are best for RSI? Can they understand the literature? Can they conduct complex scientific experiments, while maintaining good taste?
> 
> We spent about $7.4k in tokens across 8 models, 6 providers, and 3 evaluation tracks to find out.
> 
> The OpenResearch Intelligence Index evaluates models on scientific knowledge (20 hand-picked Terminal-Bench-Science tasks), post-training ability (6 novel post-training tasks), and literature relevancy/taste (a subset of PaperFindingBench), with each model running in its native harness inside OpenResearch.
> 
> So far, the index includes Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol, Gemini 4 Argon, Fable 5.1, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Kimi K3, and Qwen3.8-Flash.
> 
> Opus 5.5 scores the highest at 61.8%, costing $16.46 per task. GPT-6.1 Sol scores slightly lower (54.9%) at a fraction of the cost (only $3.83 per task). When paired with OpenResearch’s literature search tools, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash performs nearly as well as GPT-6 Astra on the literature relevancy track (47.7% vs 48.7%, respectively).
> 
> Check out the blog post to learn more: https://www.alphaxiv.org/blog/introducing-openresearch-intelligence-index. More models and source code coming soon.

📎 [Introducing the OpenResearch Intelligence Index | alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/blog/introducing-openresearch-intelligence-index)
