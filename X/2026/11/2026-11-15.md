### @hannahhaina (Hannah Xu)
[Tweet](https://x.com/hannahhaina/status/2121984694782963855)

> Data vendor friends told me lately: the fastest way into a frontier lab now is a public benchmark its models can't pass.
> 
> Hiring more experts gets you less than owning that test.
> 
> 1. Terminal-Bench 4.1 has only 66 tasks, and every frontier lab tracks it. Snorkel is one of its task authors. The best model still solves 65.8%.
> 
> 2. Halluminate published a due diligence benchmark where the best model scores 61%. Four of the top five US closed labs are paying customers.
> 
> 3. Scale AI and CAIS built Humanity's Last Exam: 2,500 questions from about 1,000 experts, with $5,000 paid for each of the top 50.
> 
> 4. Tests expire. In August Terminal-Bench cut 8 tasks, 2 of them because the newest models solved them every time.
> 
> So the scarce thing is a problem no model can solve yet, and you need a new one every few months.
> 
> If you run a data company: did your first lab deal come from a sales call, or from a benchmark?

### @HowToPrompt__ (How To Prompt)
[Tweet](https://x.com/HowToPrompt__/status/2121886161677262890)

> Researchers published a paper that might end the looped transformers.
> 
> They took a dusty 1980s neural network, retrained it in 42 milliseconds, and it’s 1,000x faster than modern transformers at reasoning.
> 
> Right now, if you want an AI to solve a hard problem, you use a "looped model". To figure out a puzzle, the AI predicts a step, feeds it back in, predicts the next step, and repeats.
> 
> It works. But it is wildly inefficient. It takes massive compute to train and burns insane amounts of FLOPs to run.
> 
> Now, a new paper just introduced a completely different way for AI to think.
> 
> It’s called CRAM (Compact Reasoning Associative Memory).
> 
> Instead of blindly predicting the next step in a loop, CRAM uses "Classical Energies".
> 
> It looks at the problem and learns an "energy landscape". Then, it naturally descends toward the lowest-energy state. It actively searches for the right answer instead of just hoping to predict it.
> The results rewrite the math on AI efficiency:
> 
> • Trains at least 1,000x faster than looped models.
> 
> • Uses 282x fewer inference FLOPs at the exact same accuracy.
> 
> • Solves 99.996% of extreme Sudoku logic puzzles.
> 
> • Compute automatically adapts to difficulty, the search stops exactly when it finds the solution.
> 
> But here is the most insane part.
> 
> When you look inside CRAM, it is not a black box.Because it learns through explicit energy states, you can actually open up the AI and inspect the learned weights.The AI explicitly learns the exact "rules" of the puzzle, and you can literally read them.
> 
> We have spent the last year throwing billions of dollars at massive GPU clusters to force language models to loop their way to logic.
> 
> This paper proves you don't need a massive supercomputer to teach an AI how to reason.
> 
> You just need better physics.
