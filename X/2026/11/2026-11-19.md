### @gurtej__gill_ (Gill)
[Tweet](https://x.com/gurtej__gill_/status/2123318423459680748)

> Great paper on teaching reasoning AI models to control real robots using only simulation, without any human demonstrations.
> 
> Right now, most engineers are either grinding through expensive human teleoperation data to train VLAs, or relying on massive closed models like GPT-6 Astra to plan actions.
> 
> These authors tried something much cleaner.
> 
> They took an open-weight 27B reasoning model (Qwen3.8-27B) and trained it purely via reinforcement learning inside simulation.
> 
> No human demonstrations, no real world training data, and no custom control harness.
> 
> The setup mirrors how computer-use agents work.
> 
> The model takes in camera frames, arm states, and a text prompt describing the workspace, reasons through the physical steps in a chain of thought, and directly outputs the next target end-effector pose.
> 
> Because it outputs hardware-agnostic poses rather than raw joint commands, standard inverse kinematics and controllers handle the actual motion.
> 
> They built a post-training engine called PEAR to run this RL at scale across simulators like LIBERO and RoboDojo.
> 
> The great thing isn't just that LIBERO success jumped from 1.6% to 74.4% (matching closed frontier models), but how well the reasoning actually generalizes.
> 
> Unlike imitation-trained models that tend to replay memorized paths and break when you change the instruction or move objects around, this model actually reads the scene and adapts.
> 
> Even wilder, when they dropped it onto a real-world dual-arm YAM robot it had never seen during training, it succeeded 60% of the time zero-shot, even sketching drawings on a whiteboard, without losing any of its general coding, math or vision capabilities.
> 
> It makes a compelling case that "robot-use RL" can just be another post-training stage for foundation models, right alongside coding and computer use.
> 
> Definitely worth a read if you're working on RL or sim-to-real.
> 
> Paper PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18562

📎 [Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18562)

### @rohanpaul_ai (Rohan Paul)
[Tweet](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2123288811069136917)

> Synthetic Sciences, a Y Combinator startup, has launched FrontierML, a benchmark so hard that none of the 5 frontier AI models it tested hit the strong-result bar on any of its 25 ML research problems.
> 
> They hide each objective, so agents must build their own tests and decide how much to trust them.
> 
> This is a test of whether AI agents can do machine learning research on their own without ever seeing the score they’ll be graded on.
> 
> Each agent gets 12 hours and real GPUs, and has to work out for itself how to measure progress.
> 
> FrontierML runs each task as a loop with the objective kept out of reach:
> 
> > The agent gets an open-ended ML problem and 12 hours in Lab0
> > It designs a local proxy for progress
> > It runs experiments on H100s and calibrates how far that proxy can be trusted
> > It submits a model or method
> > FrontierML scores it on a held-out evaluation
> 
> Synthetic Sciences argues step 3 is where much of empirical research happens. Here, a lot of the score rides on it.
