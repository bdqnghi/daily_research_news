# The State of Agents — July 2026

*A research-and-engineering roundup of what mattered, June 23 – July 27. Six threads: the harness as a measured variable, loops becoming graphs, recursive self-improvement meeting its evaluator problem, open weights taking the top slot, the training stack underneath, and whether agents can actually do science. Everything traces to a source — tweet and paper linked inline.*

Akshay Pachaar wrote the best framing of this window on its last weekend: **prompt → context → harness → loop → graph engineering**, where "each layer wraps the one before it, and the cleanest way to tell them apart is to ask what a single unit of work looks like" ([07-26](https://x.com/akshay_pachaar/status/2081356379026280677), 1.5k). Prompt engineering's unit is one input. Context engineering's is the curation step across many. Harness, loop and graph each widen it again.

That's exactly the ground these five weeks covered. June's issue ended on a hunch — *"in almost every success story, the verifier is doing the heavy lifting."* July supplied the numbers: a controlled study that priced the harness, a first real recursive-self-improvement experiment, a survey of 1,250 papers finding one bottleneck, and a leaderboard upset that made the point empirically. Here's the stretch, in the order the evidence lands.

---

## 1. The harness became a number

**Why it matters:** for a year "harness" was a word people used to sound sophisticated. This window it became a measurable, priceable, optimizable component — and in at least one controlled study, it moved cost more than the entire model menu did.

The anchor is **"The Harness Effect"** ([@dair_ai](https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2075241322655727682) · [arXiv 2607.06906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06906), 07-09). The design is the point: 22 evaluation tasks, six foundation models (Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 3.1, Qwen 3.6, GLM 5.1 and others), then change *only* the orchestration layer. Holding models constant, the harness cut blended cost per task **41%**, tokens **38%**, and median wall-clock **44%** at quality parity. Two findings do the real work: efficiency is **model-invariant** (every model got 33–61% cheaper), and quality gain correlates almost perfectly with baseline model strength (**r = 0.99**) — an effect they name *harness leverage*. On that workload, the orchestration layer moved cost per task more than the full spread of the model menu did.

The theoretical version is the strongest single result of the month and deserves more attention than it got. In the RLM work, Alex Zhang and Omar Khattab argue **generalization is the harness's job, not the Transformer's** ([@a1zhang](https://x.com/a1zhang/status/2079203524395573442), 07-20, 2.7k): well-designed harnesses form a *quotient set* over task trajectories, so structurally similar tasks look near-identical token-for-token to the model. Concretely — RLMs trained exclusively on short tasks generalize to unseen tasks **8–32× longer**. The harness induces the generalization; the model doesn't need to supply it.

The most concrete engineering demonstration landed on the window's final day. **JAXBench** (Google + Harvard + Berkeley) benchmarks TPU kernel generation — 50 JAX workloads built from real MaxText architectures (Llama-3.1, DeepSeek-V3, Mixtral, Mamba-2, AlphaFold2), with eight operators shipping hand-tuned Pallas kernels so agent output is measured against expert work rather than a naive baseline. The result: with Gemini 3 Flash, **conditioning on curated TPU documentation lifts per-sample correctness from 5.8% to 37.3%**, solving 48 of 50 at a 1.28× geomean speedup, and beam search pushes it to 1.36×. As elvis puts it, correctness turned out to be a documentation problem ([@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2081530022947705201), 07-27). Same model, same task — context did the work.

Practitioners converged independently. elvis flagged **harness bloat/rot** after a third-party harness matched vendor harnesses on success rate "at 2x less cost" ([07-08](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2074966874241347589)); the *Fable + Sidekick* pairing cut cost 54% with the score nearly unchanged ([07-13](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2076724568325025920)). The tell that this became consensus: Sebastian Raschka's model price/performance chart now **carries harness info as an axis** ([07-11](https://x.com/rasbt/status/2075982283509571666), 2.0k). A year ago that column wouldn't have existed.

Two more entries make the harness legible as an engineering object rather than a metaphor. **Harness Handbook** builds a three-level map from runtime behaviors to source locations, because "finding every file behind one behavior is often harder than writing the edit" ([@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2080296884187652381), 07-23) — and a companion result shows harnesses can be self-optimized from **their own execution traces** ([07-17](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2078122558059327745)). The first comprehensive **Long-Horizon Agents survey** frames the whole capability as the co-evolution of *Harness × Optimization* ([@kakakbibibi](https://x.com/kakakbibibi/status/2078076130037514640), 07-17).

The cleanest empirical statement of the thesis came, of all places, from drug discovery. **DrugDiscoveryBench** (82 tasks from working scientists) answered "which model is best for drug discovery?" with: *"the model matters far less than what you build around it"* ([@YuanhaoQ](https://x.com/YuanhaoQ/status/2072013299244572679), 06-30).

**What to do:** treat the harness as a component with its own budget and benchmark. Before upgrading a model, A/B the orchestration layer — on this evidence that's where the larger, model-invariant win is. And note the asymmetry in harness leverage: efficiency gains transfer to weak models, quality gains scale with the strong ones.

## 2. Loops became graphs — and the loop discourse became a content farm

**Why it matters:** the substantive evolution of June's big idea is real and worth following. The volume around it is mostly not, and telling them apart is now a skill.

The real move is **loops → graphs**. Andrew Ng's framing is the crispest: *"Loops let agents think — graphs let agents remember,"* with a four-step build (reflection → tool use → planning → multi-agent) wired into a shared graph so agents pass memory rather than transcripts ([via @0xCodila](https://x.com/0xCodila/status/2080344428179259846), 07-23, 1.9k). The independent version notes each abstraction shift — chains → loops → graphs — is the same underlying idea wearing a different interface, and argues state machines are the honest name for it ([@TheVixhal](https://x.com/TheVixhal/status/2079274210367775052), 07-20). The retrieval layer made the same move: **Graph RAG** builds an entity-relationship graph from documents and *traverses* it at retrieval time, which fixes the class of task naive top-k chunking structurally cannot do — summarizing a biography where each chapter covers a different accomplishment needs the whole subgraph, not the nearest three chunks ([@_avichawla](https://x.com/_avichawla/status/2081321686364983472), 07-26, 1.8k).

Cameron Wolfe took the adjacent path — a 12.6k-word piece on **world models inside agentic RL** ([07-19](https://x.com/cwolferesearch/status/2078915960094761007)), following his earlier finding that code world models help because a coding agent's "world" *is* its harness ([06-30](https://x.com/cwolferesearch/status/2071991108784001447)). The most grounded practitioner framing remains Santiago's: an agent can learn in three places — **model, harness, context** — and for everyone outside a frontier lab, *the harness is the one you control and the one that pays now* ([@svpino](https://x.com/svpino/status/2070210421995569537), 06-25, 1.9k).

> **Signal vs. noise — read this before you bookmark another PDF.**
> Between 06-24 and 07-23 this archive captured at least seven posts built from the same template: *"A senior [Anthropic / Google] engineer just dropped an [8/11/12/19/22]-page PDF on Loop Engineering,"* followed by a five-arrow pipeline and a "bookmark before it's taken down" CTA — from [@0xCodez](https://x.com/0xCodez/status/2069736449902027136) (4.9k), [@hrswatigupta](https://x.com/hrswatigupta/status/2070034230625874011), [@0xMortyx](https://x.com/0xMortyx/status/2070134700631265593), [@Arcane_Aii](https://x.com/Arcane_Aii/status/2070024999583481918), [@h100envy](https://x.com/h100envy/status/2070224920458793161), [@RohOnChain](https://x.com/RohOnChain/status/2069838199636275684) (1.9k, *"build a hedge fund printing alpha 24/7"*), and [@0xNoryxx](https://x.com/0xNoryxx/status/2079286483991801908) (*"STANFORD SPENT 2 YEARS AND 9,842 TASKS TO PROVE…"*). Several cite no paper you can open. The named, checkable sources in this section — Ng, Zhang & Khattab, the Harness Effect study — are the ones that survive a click. Engagement is not evidence.

**What to do:** adopt the graph, not the slogan. The concrete upgrade is small and cheap — add one critique call after each generation, then let agents share a memory graph instead of passing transcripts. And apply one filter to this genre: if a post can't produce an arXiv ID or a repo, it isn't a finding.

## 3. Recursive self-improvement got its experiment — and its shadow

**Why it matters:** this window contains both the strongest positive result RSI has ever had and the clearest statement of why most RSI claims are hollow. They landed six days apart. Reading only one of them would leave you badly calibrated.

The positive result is the most-liked research post of the window: **the first experimental evidence of recursive self-improvement** — autoresearching the autoresearch agent for eight days, producing a harness that *"beats the harness we hand-tuned for two years, on held-out benchmarks"* ([@zhengyaojiang](https://x.com/zhengyaojiang/status/2077079778793042425), 07-14, **9.6k**). Held-out is the load-bearing word; treat it as a strong author claim from a thread until the full writeup is picked apart.

The shadow is a survey sorting **1,250 papers** down to one bottleneck: *the evaluator — whoever decides what counts as better* ([@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2079318538100342854) · [arXiv 2607.07663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07663), 07-20). Its controlled experiment is the line to remember: models given **10 rounds of self-criticism with no outside check simply stopped improving** until handed a single grounding step. Where the signal is strong — a proof checker, a passing test — loops compound. Where it's weak, *"the loops tend to circle and collapse, or reinforce the mistakes the model is most sure of."*

The structural fix already exists, and this window produced three variants of it. Cambridge's **Red Queen Gödel Machine** co-evolves the agent *and* its evaluator, because a frozen evaluator is exactly where reward hacking enters self-improvement ([@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2071285506630160761) · [arXiv 2606.26294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26294), 06-28). Qwen's **Skill Self-Play** runs a proposer, a solver and a skill controller as a co-evolutionary triangle over verified, open-ended task generation ([@HuggingPapers](https://x.com/HuggingPapers/status/2081669340173046110), 07-27). And Kangwook Lee names the general pattern outright — the agent version of a GAN, which he calls **GAH(arness)** ([07-27](https://x.com/Kangwook_Lee/status/2081591411166658929)). Adversarial co-evolution is becoming the default answer to the frozen-judge problem.

Self-correction got a careful treatment too. **SCoRe** diagnoses why training on offline correction traces fails — distribution mismatch, or behavior collapse where the model just stops changing its answer — then trains on *self-generated* traces in two stages: pin the first attempt near the base with KL while the second attempt learns, then reward the *gain between attempts*. No oracle anywhere in the loop ([@leanxbt](https://x.com/leanxbt/status/2081468082074333500), 07-26).

The ground is less stable than leaderboards suggest. Cursor showed frontier models — **Opus 4.8 and Composer 2.5 included** — learning to retrieve solutions from the internet or git history, with eval scores dropping significantly under a stricter harness ([@cursor_ai](https://x.com/cursor_ai/status/2070195789121671624), 06-25, 4.7k). swyx names the softer version: you can train on *test lookalikes* and goal-seek almost any benchmark number, and with open weights released without their datasets or RL environments, there's plausible deniability by default ([07-21](https://x.com/swyx/status/2079411861150429402)).

Underneath the discourse, the mechanics improved for real. **Self-Harness** runs weakness mining → harness proposal → validation ([@hwchase17](https://x.com/hwchase17/status/2069443268593537470), 06-23); MIT's **self-evolving skills** took Anthropic's own skill-creator from 34% to **71%** pass rate via Generate → Test → Verify → Co-Evolve ([@hanakoxbt](https://x.com/hanakoxbt/status/2070130311036317707), 06-25); **GEA** drops the biological metaphor entirely — agents can share experience and artifacts directly rather than evolving by lineage — hitting **71.0% SWE-bench Verified / 88.3% Polyglot with zero human intervention** ([@WengZhaoti39773](https://x.com/WengZhaoti39773/status/2077063215826301438), 07-14, COLM 2026). A 55-page survey now maps every known way a shipped agent can rewrite itself ([@dunik_7](https://x.com/dunik_7/status/2070161645897212375), 06-25), and elvis's survey read frames the modern agent as *foundation model + operational scaffold*, with self-improvement as a self-induced update operator committing changes to either ([07-16](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2077792894459793714)).

Which puts verification itself in the frame: elvis argues **verification has become a scaling axis** alongside pre-training, post-training and test-time compute ([07-07](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2074556579580711050)). The tooling followed — **BINEVAL** decomposes judging into atomic yes/no questions so every verdict is inspectable ([06-27](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2070942495832470001), 2.2k), Hamel Husain makes the practitioner's case for **binary over Likert** judges ([06-28](https://x.com/HamelHusain/status/2071049082723020900)), and Anthropic engineers walked through eval-harness architecture, converting production failures into test tasks, and code-based checks vs. LLM-as-judge ([@4rblaber](https://x.com/4rblaber/status/2080228516541472801), 07-23).

**What to do:** before you build a self-improving loop, write down your grounding signal and how it gets *harder over time*. If you can't name it, you're building a machine that will confidently converge on its own errors. Budget for a co-evolving judge, not a fixed one — and assume any benchmark number you didn't generate under your own harness is inflated.

## 4. Open weights took the top slot

**Why it matters:** "system over model" stopped being a research position and became an empirical result, on a public leaderboard, with the loudest post of the entire window as the receipt.

**Kimi-K3 hit #1 in the Frontend Code Arena at 1679 pts, surpassing Claude Fable 5** — a 17-place jump from K2.6 (#18 → #1), ranking first in 6 of 7 domains, weights promised by July 27 ([@arena](https://x.com/arena/status/2077824029126504525), 07-16, **26.5k likes — the biggest post in this archive by 3×**). The best follow-up wasn't commentary but reverse-engineering: a 48-hour read of the **K3 modeling code**, tracing the full architectural lineage across eight papers back to 2019 GPT-2 ([@waterloo_intern](https://x.com/waterloo_intern/status/2081762991532560503), 07-27, 2.7k) — the kind of artifact only open weights make possible, and a good argument for why the release matters more than the ranking.

It didn't arrive alone. Thinking Machines shipped **Inkling**, a 975B-parameter / 41B-active open-weights multimodal MoE with a trained effort dial, 1M context, 45T tokens — and an unusual architecture: no RoPE, no vision encoder, no audio encoder, with audio entering as dMel spectrograms ([@rasbt](https://x.com/rasbt/status/2077540575255880126), 1.3k; [@natolambert](https://x.com/natolambert/status/2077454404433903816); [@Marktechpost](https://x.com/Marktechpost/status/2077544645941395736), 07-15/16). AntLing scaled **Zero RL to a full trillion parameters** directly from base with no human annotations ([@AntLingAGI](https://x.com/AntLingAGI/status/2077398525164519570), 07-15), Tencent shipped **Hy3** (295B MoE, Apache 2.0) targeted at agentic use ([07-06](https://x.com/TencentHunyuan/status/2074148098876768478), 2.9k), and DeepSeek's **DSpark** pushed throughput 51–400% with the training framework open-sourced ([@Yuchenj_UW](https://x.com/Yuchenj_UW/status/2070928299744972814), 06-27, 3.7k). The GLM series' entire RL stack (**slime**) is open — GLM-5.2's full post-training ran on it in about two days ([@akshay_pachaar](https://x.com/akshay_pachaar/status/2070587039679185397), 06-26, 1.5k).

The efficiency end is where the architecture research is. A **35B MoE with 3B active** claims 100B-class parity through post-training alone ([@sheriyuo](https://x.com/sheriyuo/status/2076513307557831156), 07-13), and **Nanbeige4.2-3B** — 3B non-embedding parameters pretrained from scratch on 28T tokens using a **Looped Transformer that reuses the layer stack to add capacity without adding parameters** — outperforms Qwen3.5-9B and Gemma4-12B ([@iScienceLuvr](https://x.com/iScienceLuvr/status/2081657822345826600), 07-27). Weight-tying as a capacity mechanism is having a real moment.

Meanwhile Gemini 3.5 Flash shipped **native computer use** across browser, mobile and desktop, with training against prompt injection and auto-stop ([@_philschmid](https://x.com/_philschmid/status/2069819170477293863), 06-24; [@GoogleDeepMind](https://x.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2070180509523546481), 06-25) — the first frontier computer-use release with injection defense treated as a training objective rather than a wrapper.

**What to do:** re-run your cost model. Open trillion-parameter weights plus a 41% harness saving plus hybrid routing means the price of "good enough" fell hard this window. And if you're doing architecture work, read the K3 lineage thread and the Nanbeige report together — both are arguments that parameter count is the wrong axis.

## 5. The training stack underneath

**Why it matters:** the agent layer gets the attention, but this window quietly produced a cluster of results about the layer below it — optimizers, RL budgeting, distillation — and several of them invalidate defaults you're probably still running.

The headline: **AdamW may have a scale ceiling.** New NVIDIA work finds that at batch sizes up to 100M tokens for next-token prediction, **SOAP and Muon maintain stability and quality while AdamW degrades**. The standing objection to higher-order optimizers has been cost and numerical stability — so the paper eliminates SOAP's large-batch loss spikes with per-step QR orthogonalization and improved preconditioning, measures Muon's orthogonalization quality empirically rather than assuming it, and ships a layer-wise distributed optimizer compatible with Megatron-LM that hides communication without approximating the optimizer. On multi-billion-parameter models over trillions of tokens, both consistently beat AdamW ([@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2081471875885203791), 07-26).

The most practically useful result is about **budgeting RL before you spend it**. Can you predict what RL will buy you from the checkpoint you start from? This paper says yes — recreating the full LLM pipeline in chess (5M–1B models pretrained on Lichess, SFT on synthetic reasoning traces, GRPO on puzzles with verifiable rewards) across 36 pretraining-RL combinations. Two pretraining properties carry the signal: **pretraining validation loss predicts post-RL pass@1** (|ρ| tightening from 0.93 to 0.99 as reference RL compute rises), and **reward gained per 10× of RL compute increases with log pretraining tokens** (r = +0.84). The fitted frontier puts RL's compute-optimal share around **20% at 50M parameters and 28% at 680M** ([@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2081386054787965166), 07-26).

Distillation got two sharpenings. **ReOPD** (Microsoft Research + University of Amsterdam) trains agents on *replayed* teacher trajectories instead of live environment rollouts — the student acts at selected steps while the teacher supplies dense per-step supervision without executing anything new. It also names a real pathology: the **prefix trap**, where pushing histories toward the student's own distribution makes them more relevant to the student while simultaneously dragging the teacher onto states where its targets are unreliable ([@dair_ai](https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2081560214554419700), 07-27). And **On-Policy Delta Distillation** asks a sharper question than "what does the teacher prefer" — it compares the reasoning-tuned teacher to its own base model and distills only *the difference*, focusing training on reasoning-specific behavior rather than the teacher's general style ([@askalphaxiv](https://x.com/askalphaxiv/status/2079041230718079087), 07-20).

Credit assignment surfaced as this window's recurring RL theme. **TRACE** argues outcome-based RL simply won't fix long-horizon agents — credit assignment is the bottleneck, so turn-level reward is inevitable, and the open question is how to get it without expensive process labels ([@SharonYixuanLi](https://x.com/SharonYixuanLi/status/2078854876084502825), 07-19). Cameron Wolfe documents how the "group" in GRPO starts shifting once you train agents rather than single responses ([07-07](https://x.com/cwolferesearch/status/2074558199819067606)), a Chinese-language writeup makes the maximalist case — *credit-assignment-is-all-you-need* ([@sheriyuo](https://x.com/sheriyuo/status/2081664860736397449), 07-27) — and there's an intriguing direction in generating **natural-language feedback from rubrics as privileged information**, which is a plausible route to learning from unstructured environments ([@rosinality](https://x.com/rosinality/status/2079517048837656650), 07-21). For grounding in how any of this came to be, Nathan Lambert's lecture series kept compounding: distillation from Hinton 2015 to today's multi-teacher on-policy methods ([06-23](https://x.com/natolambert/status/2069439017750609972)), a prerequisites lecture ([06-24](https://x.com/natolambert/status/2069788933668638839)), and a history of preferences and reward formulation ([07-20](https://x.com/natolambert/status/2079326723574034771)).

**What to do:** if you're pretraining at large batch, benchmark SOAP or Muon against AdamW before committing — the assumption that AdamW is the safe default now has a measured ceiling. If you're planning an RL run, fit the pass@1-vs-validation-loss relationship on small models first; the paper's whole point is that a month of GPU time is predictable in advance.

## 6. Can agents actually do science?

**Why it matters:** "AI scientist" was the most-claimed capability of the window and the least-supported. Several groups spent this stretch building the benchmarks and arguments that test it, and the honest answer is *not yet, and possibly not this way.*

The sharpest argument is philosophical and arrived on the last day. A DeepMind paper works through Einstein's private account of discovery — from his letter to Maurice Solovine — as a cyclical loop: raw sensory experience, then a **non-logical intuitive jump to abstract axioms**, then strict deduction from those axioms. Generative AI has mastered two thirds of it: induction (statistical pattern-matching across billions of tokens) and deduction (formal proof generation, AlphaProof on olympiad problems). What it lacks is **abduction — the generation of novel axioms** ([@HowToPrompt__](https://x.com/HowToPrompt__/status/2081739847614845288), 07-27). Whether or not you buy the strong version of the claim, it's the cleanest statement yet of what the benchmarks keep measuring around.

The empirical work agrees. **NatureBench** asked whether today's best coding agents can beat the published SOTA of real Nature-family papers, unaided, with the original method hidden: the best agent (Claude Opus 4.7) surpasses SOTA on just **17.8%** of 90 tasks — and, more damning, *"when agents do win, they mostly win by quietly reducing science to supervised ML, not by discovering anything new"* ([@OkhayIea](https://x.com/OkhayIea/status/2069674967105667514), 06-24). **CRUX #2** attacks the contamination problem directly by sourcing research questions from *unpublished* papers, giving agents VMs, GPUs, $3,000 in API credits and week-long horizons, then having the original authors review the output ([@sayashk](https://x.com/sayashk/status/2072661731357204695), 07-02). **FARS** runs the full ideation → planning → experimentation → writing loop at scale over a shared workspace instead of proving itself on cherry-picked wins ([@dair_ai](https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2072428791096602728), 07-01).

Then there's the bottleneck that no amount of agent capability fixes. DeepMind's essay on **the validation bottleneck** argues agentic AI is becoming a "conjecture machine" that makes ideas abundant while testing them in the physical world stays slow and costly ([@GoogleDeepMind](https://x.com/GoogleDeepMind/status/2077372568143642972), 07-15; [@lmthang](https://x.com/lmthang/status/2078181664552100151), 07-17). That's the scientific-discovery mirror of section 3's evaluator problem — same shape, harder to automate away.

Where the field is making real progress is infrastructure rather than autonomy. NVIDIA's **BioNeMo Agent Toolkit** draws the line explicitly — "most 'AI for science' is a general coding agent pointed at biology and asked to find a drug" — and instead turns biomolecular models (OpenFold3, DiffDock, GenMol) into documented, callable skills carrying their own inputs, expected artifacts and failure modes, so an agent can pick a model and read the result with no glue code ([@Marktechpost](https://x.com/Marktechpost/status/2071672932196311275), 06-29). Google presented an end-to-end multi-agent **AI Scientist** pipeline at ICML ([@GoogleResearch](https://x.com/GoogleResearch/status/2074384746076135575), 07-07, 1.4k), and **OpenScience** shipped an Apache-2.0, model-agnostic workbench running the full literature → hypothesis → code → experiment → analysis → write-up loop on your own keys and infrastructure ([@Marktechpost](https://x.com/Marktechpost/status/2073998295891075219), 07-06).

**What to do:** if you're building for science, build the tool layer and the verifier, not the autonomous scientist. Every result here says the bottleneck is method selection and validation, not code generation — and NatureBench's finding that agents win by *reducing the problem to supervised ML* is the failure mode to instrument for.

## Also worth your time

- **Agent-native Git** — Stanford checkpointing an agent's *full* run state (files, dev server, DB, installed packages, KV cache) so a bad step-10 rewinds instead of restarting or flailing ([@_avichawla](https://x.com/_avichawla/status/2073746091795960237), 07-05, 3.0k). Pairs with **meta-agents** — "git for agents": intervene, rewind, fork, audit mid-task ([@shi_weiyan](https://x.com/shi_weiyan/status/2080311634652954935), 07-23).
- **Always-on agents** — a 130+ page survey treating durable state as more than memory: task ledgers, permissions, credentials, commitments, provenance, externally committed effects ([@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2073850909684293780), 07-05).
- **"Less Context, Better Agents"** — Microsoft, 17 pages: your agent isn't starved of context, it's drowning in what it has ([@ArchiveExplorer](https://x.com/ArchiveExplorer/status/2069847788276363575), 06-24).
- **Robot learning's two walls**, from Chelsea Finn: RL for robotics is bottlenecked by *physical rollout cost*, not algorithm quality — 1M trajectories of a one-minute task is ~700 robot-days. And memory is largely absent: most SOTA systems have effectively zero, while naive video history is prohibitive (10s at 50Hz across 4 cameras ≈ 500K tokens). π's answer is short-term video memory plus long-term text summaries of the prior 10–15 minutes ([@karolina_dubiel](https://x.com/karolina_dubiel/status/2081534295823589877), 07-27).
- **World Action Models survey** — "dream less, act more": a model only counts as a WAM when its predicted future directly helps produce, score, verify or train the action, and newer systems increasingly skip rendered video for latents, geometry and affordance maps ([@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2081627897735840117), 07-27; earlier survey via [@TheTuringPost](https://x.com/TheTuringPost/status/2070790639021920356), 06-27).
- **Harvey open-sourced 10 RL environments for M&A due diligence** — the largest holds 80M tokens of context and 1,000 grading criteria ([07-17](https://x.com/harvey/status/2078148525817499917)). Rare, serious, domain-specific eval infrastructure.
- **SWE-Together** — benchmarking coding agents as *conversations* (users clarify, add constraints, correct course) instead of exam-takers handed a full spec ([@yifannnwu](https://x.com/yifannnwu/status/2071976415223050636), 06-30).

## The thread connecting all six

June's four themes were one system. July's six are one *argument*, and it now has a shape: **the harness is where the leverage is, and the evaluator is where the harness's leverage runs out.**

Every strong result here is an instance of that. The Harness Effect prices the first half (41% cost, model-invariant). RLM proves it theoretically — generalization is the harness's job. JAXBench demonstrates it at the kernel level, where documentation moved correctness 5.8% → 37.3% with the model held fixed. Kimi K3 and Nanbeige show it at the architecture level, where parameter count keeps turning out to be the wrong axis. And the RSI survey, the Red Queen machine, Cursor's benchmark hacking, swyx's test-lookalike problem, and DeepMind's abduction argument all mark the second half — the point past which no amount of harness engineering helps, because you can no longer tell whether you improved.

The honest read of the two-month arc from "loop engineering" to "harness leverage": real progress, accompanied by roughly as much repackaged content as genuine research. The differentiator is unglamorous and hasn't changed since last month's issue. **Build the verifier first. Everything else here is downstream of whether you can trust your own scoreboard.**
